# Organizing Quality

**Organizing quality** means deciding, for a project and for the organization around it, which quality work is done, by whom, at what cost, and how the way of working improves over time. The pages below take one question each; the gurus behind the ideas (Deming, Juran, Crosby) are on [Quality Gurus](../define/foundations/gurus.md).

| Section | The question it answers |
|---|---|
| [Cost of Quality](01-coq/) | What do prevention, appraisal and failure cost, and where are defects made and found? |
| [Quality Planning](02-plan/) | Which verification and validation activities does this project need, with which gates and effort? |
| [Process Improvement](03-process/) | How does a team find the causes of its defects and change its process without making things worse first? |
| [Quality Standards](04-standards/) | What do CMMI, ITIL, ISO 9000 and TMMi ask for, and what does adopting them achieve? |
| [Industry Practices](05-practice/) | How do DevOps, SRE, retrospectives and companies such as Google, Microsoft and Facebook organize quality? |

The sections build on one another. Cost-of-quality data show where defects are made, where they are found and what each escape costs; a quality plan uses that picture to choose activities and gates for one project. Process improvement then asks why the same defects keep appearing and changes the way of working. Standards and maturity models describe what a whole organization should have in place, and the industry pages show how large companies actually run it, with measured results and their limits.

A reader new to the topic can start with [CoSQ Foundations](01-coq/01-cosq-foundations.md): its four cost categories are the vocabulary the other sections use.
